TIME

Temporally Intelligent Meta-reasoning Engine for Context-Triggered Explicit Reasoning

paper ↗code & training data ↗TIMEBench ↗

I wanted a model that could decide to reason explicitly when the conversation actually called for it halfway through an answer, instead of committing to one global thinking mode up front.

What bugged me

“Thinking mode” is oddly all-or-nothing.

Many reasoning models make the choice once at the beginning: produce a long visible reasoning block, or don’t. That felt unnecessarily rigid.

Conversations keep changing.

Time passes, assumptions go stale, users return later, and new information can suddenly make an earlier line of reasoning worth revisiting. Temporal cues gave me a clean way to probe that.

What I tried

TIME treats explicit reasoning as a context-triggered policy. I added lightweight dialogue primitives for elapsed time and silent time passage, then trained Qwen3 models from 4B through 32B with a four-stage supervised curriculum. The goal was not to make a clock-reading model; temporality is a controlled source of contextual change.

How I tested it

I built TIMEBench: 77 scenarios across seven categories, with ten trials per scenario per model. It is meant to diagnose reasoning under temporal and latent contextual change rather than temporal fact recall.
