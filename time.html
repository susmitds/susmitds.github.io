TIME

Temporally Intelligent Meta-reasoning Engine for Context-Triggered Explicit Reasoning

Paper ↗ Code & training data ↗ TIMEBench ↗

The core idea: explicit reasoning does not have to be a global “thinking mode.” It can be learned as a control policy that appears when the conversational context makes a re-check, inference, or re-anchoring useful.

Why treat reasoning as a policy?

Fixed modes are blunt

Reasoning models often either emit a long front-loaded trace or suppress explicit reasoning entirely. That spends tokens even when little reasoning is needed and makes it difficult to re-enter a reasoning phase once the answer is underway.

Context changes inside a conversation

Deadlines move, assumptions go stale, users return after long gaps, and new information can invalidate what was reasonable a few turns earlier. TIME uses temporality as a controlled probe for that latent state change.

What TIME changes

TIME adds three lightweight primitives to dialogue. The point is not to make timestamps the task; the timestamps and silent time shifts create structured cues that can require the model to reconsider context.

<time>

Optional ISO-8601 timestamps make elapsed time and delayed returns visible in the conversation.

<think>

Short reasoning bursts can appear anywhere in a response, including more than once, instead of only as one front-loaded block.

tick

A turn containing only time metadata represents silent passage of time without inventing additional user content.

I trained Qwen3 models from 4B to 32B with a four-phase supervised curriculum. The final stage is deliberately small and maximally diverse: the recurring invariant is the reasoning policy itself rather than topic, style, or formatting.

TIMEBench

TIMEBench is a diagnostic benchmark for reasoning from temporal cues and latent contextual change, not a temporal fact-recall benchmark. It contains 77 scenarios across seven categories, with 10 trials per scenario for each model.

77scenario-level diagnostics
770evaluation runs per model
4B–32BQwen3 scales evaluated
7diagnostic categories

The categories probe chronological retrospection, invalid-time detection, temporal adaptivity, temporal contextual awareness, temporal-flow anomalies, time-gap awareness, and timezone sensitivity.

What changed after alignment?

Across all four model sizes, TIME outperformed the corresponding Qwen3 thinking and no-thinking baselines on TIMEBench. The behavioral shift is at least as important as the score: explicit reasoning becomes shorter, more selective, and usually appears in the middle of the response rather than only at the beginning.

The point is not simply “less chain of thought.” TIME is trying to change the policy governing when explicit reasoning appears and whether it can be re-triggered as context evolves.
