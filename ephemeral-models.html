Ephemeral Models, Durable Claims

Closed-Model Dependence and the Preservation of AI Research

Short public explainer · manuscript not linked during review

The core question: what happens to the scientific record when an experiment depends on a model that the authors cannot archive, and the provider can later change, deprecate, or retire?

The preservation problem

A retainable model

If the exact executable artifact can be kept locally, researchers can often preserve it alongside code, prompts, data, and evaluation scripts.

A provider-controlled model

If access exists only through an API or product, researchers can record identifiers and inputs, but continued access to the computational artifact remains outside the scholarly archive.

A replacement model can support a new experiment, but it cannot recreate the historical object that generated, judged, or served as a baseline for the original evidence.

What I measured

I built a frozen census of 53,538 final archival papers across ACL, EMNLP, ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS from 2023–2026, then measured whether covered closed models appeared in four non-exclusive scientific roles. Writing, coding, translation, and similar assistance are tracked separately and do not count toward the primary measure.

DirectThe model is an experimental object, target, backbone, agent, or system being studied.
BaselineIts result is part of the comparison used to motivate or interpret a claim.
JudgeThe model scores, ranks, critiques, or otherwise evaluates another system’s output.
Data generationThe model generates, annotates, labels, or constructs experimental material.

How the measurement works

The pipeline was designed to keep high-recall retrieval, deterministic evidence rules, semantic adjudication, validation, and lifecycle curation separate. Only unresolved entities are sent to a locally served LLM adjudicator; deterministic decisions cannot be overwritten by it.

The dependence is not confined to comparison tables. Closed models increasingly participate in data construction and evaluation, and model lifecycles can move faster than the archival record that cites them.

What the paper does and does not claim

This is not a paper-level “irreproducibility rate.” It measures where recent archival research depends on computational artifacts whose identity or future availability is controlled outside the archive.

The paper distinguishes three preservation failures: drift when the reference still resolves but behavior changes, extinction when the model or snapshot is no longer callable, and underspecification when the paper never identifies one fixed served artifact in the first place.

The practical implication is fairly modest: when a locally retainable model can serve the same scientific role, preservation comes almost for free. When a closed model is necessary, exact identifiers, access dates, material settings, inputs/outputs, and post-commercial research access become part of preserving the evidence.
